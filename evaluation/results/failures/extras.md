Units redrawn: 72 test people with detector windows (plus 618 windows of synthetic movement only, each its own unit); 35 people with marked-tremor corrector sequences. Distances here are over the whole sequence after 2.56 s (moving and still together).

## B. Failures per person (detector)

- **missed tremor windows** (people only, 35 people with such windows, 122 failures): 5 people have none; the 5 people with the most cause 37% of them; 8 people cause half. Worst person: 11 of 108 windows.
- **false alarms** (people only, 72 people with such windows, 688 failures): 0 people have none; the 5 people with the most cause 12% of them; 25 people cause half. Worst person: 19 of 48 windows.

## C. Corrector worse than no filter on REAL marked tremor

19 of 236 test sequences with marked tremor end up further from the intended path with our system than with no filter at all.

- `corrector_worse_tremor_1.png`: ours 4.45 px, no filter 1.18 px; Parkinson's, rest, tremor 1.3 px at 5.1 Hz; mean gate 0.83
- `corrector_worse_tremor_2.png`: ours 4.19 px, no filter 1.16 px; Parkinson's, rest, tremor 1.2 px at 8.2 Hz; mean gate 0.88

Worse than no filter, by tremor size: mild (under 3 px) 7 of 47; medium (3-10 px) 10 of 88; strong (over 10 px) 2 of 101.

## D. The demo page's weak spot: a hand held perfectly still

Medium replayed tremor of test people, hand still, after a 3 s warm-up (server code, as `compare_modes.py`): average distance from the hold point: no filter 6.08 px, One Euro 1.62 px, our model 1.52 px; our correction was more than half on for 92% of the time.
