About these numbers

"Tremor-like movement" means the pointer shook in a steady rhythm of 3 to 12 times a second. It does not mean a diagnosis, and changes from day to day can have many causes.

How it works

Tremor is fast and rhythmic. Pointing at something is slow. That difference in speed is what lets a computer tell them apart.

  1. Read

    While you move inside the practice area, the page sends the pointer's position to a small program running on this laptop.

  2. Detect

    The first model looks at the last 2.56 seconds of movement and gives a score from 0 to 1 for how much it looks like tremor: a steady rhythm of 3 to 12 shakes a second. That score is the tremor meter.

  3. Correct

    The second model is a small neural network. It works out where the pointer was meant to be, using only movement that has already happened: it never waits to see what comes next. The detector decides how much of that correction is applied, so a steady hand is left alone.

How well it does

Both models were trained only on public and computer-generated data. The people used for the final test were never seen during training.

What happens to your movement

Both models run on this laptop. The assistant only sees the summary numbers, never your raw movement. You can delete the saved data from the assistant window.

This project is a prototype and is not medical advice.
